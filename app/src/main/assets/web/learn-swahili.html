Learn Swahili: The Unifying Voice of East Africa

Kiswahili is the official working language of the African Union and the daily tongue of 200 million people from the Indian Ocean coast to Central Africa. Unlike many Niger-Congo languages, Swahili is non-tonal!

1. The Beauty of Noun Classes (Ngeli)

Instead of grammatical gender (he/she/it), Swahili groups nouns into logical semantic categories that synchronize prefixes across verbs and adjectives:

Class 1/2 (Human beings): Mtu (Person) → Watu (People)

Class 7/8 (Objects/Tools): Kitabu (Book) → Vitabu (Books)

Example Concord: Mtu mmoja anasoma (One person is reading) vs Kitabu kimoja kinaanguka (One book is falling).

2. Respectful Greetings & Daily Courtesies

  • Shikamoo — Respectful greeting strictly reserved for elders (Literally: "I hold your feet").
  • Marahaba — The gracious reply from the elder.
  • Habari gani? / Habari yako? — What is the news? / How are you?
  • Nzuri sana — Very good / Peaceful.
  • Asante sana — Thank you very much.
  • Karibu tena — You are welcome again.
