Learn Igbo: Vowel Harmony, Kola Traditions, and Tones

Spoken by 30 million people in southeastern Nigeria and worldwide, Igbo is a dynamic Niger-Congo language celebrated for its democratic entrepreneurship, intricate proverbs (Ilu), and sacred Kola nut ceremonies.

1. The Secret to Igbo: Vowel Harmony

Igbo vowels are strictly divided into two distinct phonetic families based on Advanced Tongue Root (ATR):

Light (+ATR) Vowels:

a, e, i, o, u

Tongue root pushed forward, crisp resonance.

Heavy (-ATR) Dotted Vowels:

ị, ọ, ụ

Subdot indicates retracted tongue root and deeper throat pitch.

Linguistic Rule: A standard Igbo root word rarely mixes dotted and non-dotted vowels within the same stem!

2. Essential Cultural Greetings

  • Nnọọ — Welcome (Said with open hands to incoming guests)
  • Kedụ? / Kedụ ka ị mere? — How are you?
  • Ọ dị mma — It is good / I am well
  • Daalụ — Thank you
  • Ka ọ dị — Until next time (Goodbye)

3. The Kola Nut (Ọjị) Cultural Tradition

"Onye wetara ọjị, wetara ndụ" — He who brings the kola nut brings life. In our B1 and B2 courses, you learn the sacred prayers, blessings, and formal rhetoric spoken before the kola nut is broken and shared among guests.
